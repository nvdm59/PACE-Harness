# Gate lifecycle error rates: v1 vs v2, with and without a held-out gate slice

## 0. Findings

(Authored from the tables below; every number is from sections 1–4. v1 seed 0 is bit-identical to the committed run: 8 PROMOTE / 32 REVERT, first promotion day 13.)

1. **v2 without holdout is the most permissive configuration and the only one with a material unacceptable-promotion rate for cgrpo**: 22.8 ± 1.5 promotions per 40 days (v1: 8.4 ± 2.5), lowest regret of any learned arm (0.70 ± 0.11, vs never_verify 0.74 ± 0.08 and v1 1.34 ± 0.25), but 1.0 ± 0.4 unacceptable promotions per run and P(≥1) = 3/5 seeds (v1: 1/5). Every one of the 7 unacceptable promotions in the whole grid is **over budget by a small margin** (held-out total cost $0.0951–$0.1012 against B + tol = $0.095; none regressed quality); they were served for 0–5 days each (v2: 1.6 ± 0.7 days per run).
2. **The overlap guard does what it should on AWR and almost nothing on cgrpo.** AWR candidates (per-decision ESS/n ≈ 0.21–0.28) are UNEVALUABLE in 23.8 ± 3.2 of 40 gates under v2, cgrpo candidates in 0 (ESS/n ≈ 0.63–0.77). The clip fraction is never binding (≤ 1%); the ESS floor is the active criterion.
3. **The holdout (30% gate slice) trades power for safety.** It removes the unacceptable promotions under v2 (0/5 seeds) but cuts cgrpo promotions to 7.8 ± 3.2 (v2) / 1.8 ± 0.2 (v1), delays the first promotion (9.2 / 10.0 days vs 3.6 / 5.4) and raises regret (v2_h30 1.52 ± 0.33). False rejections dominate every configuration: REVERTs of acceptable candidates are 14.2 ± 2.1 (v2) to 34.6 ± 0.7 (v1_h30) of 40.
4. **The joint difference interval is far narrower than v1's candidate interval** (cgrpo: 0.085 ± 0.009 vs 0.194 ± 0.021 at the same n), which is where v2's power comes from: the candidate and incumbent DR estimates share most of their noise.
5. **Coverage against the held-out reference is below nominal everywhere, and for cost most of the shortfall is the reference's own noise.** The held-out reference is a 150-task Monte Carlo mean with per-task cost SD ≈ $0.039, i.e. SE ≈ $0.0032, larger than the gate's total-cost interval half-width (≈ $0.0026 for cgrpo under v2). With independent noise of that size a correctly calibrated 90% interval would cover the reference only ≈ 56% of the time, which is what is observed (50–69%). The same noise makes the over-budget labels of finding 1 marginal (all within ≈ 1–2 reference SEs of the threshold). The quality-difference interval covers 70% (v2) / 84% (v2_h30) for cgrpo; for AWR without holdout it covers 19% (the trained-on-same-data candidate is over-estimated), 86% with the holdout: in-sample gating is the main source of AWR's estimator bias.
6. **Runtime.** All arms (`--arms all`, needed to keep v1 seed 0 identical to the committed run), 7 runs in parallel on 8 cores: 193–714 s wall per run (v1 configurations are slowest because they also compute the shadow v2 record). Total grid ≈ 35 min. No arms were dropped.

Default schedule, 40 days, seeds 0, 1, 2, 3, 4; `--arms all` (so v1 seed 0 is the committed run), reported arms: continual (cgrpo), continual_awr, never_verify, always_verify; the per-stage oracle is the regret reference. Runtime per run 413 s mean (192–713 s), single-threaded NumPy. Generated by `python -m pace.experiments.mas_continual.gate_lifecycle report`.

Gate counts are over all 40 days (the gate runs from day 1); regret is days 6–40. Coverage is the share of gates whose 90% interval contains the held-out reference value, **against the held-out reference**, which itself carries Monte Carlo error; it is not coverage of an exact truth.
